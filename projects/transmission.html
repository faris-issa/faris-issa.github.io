Shift Happens
Six-speed manual transmission

We designed a hand-cranked transmission in SolidWorks and built it using 3D-printed components and aluminum shafts.

Our goal was to build a working gearbox that could demonstrate gear selection, power transfer, and the relationship between speed and torque.

Engineering process

From concept to prototype

01

System definition

We mapped the power transfer and gear selection, identified critical components, and considered potential issues with shaft deflection, gear wear and alignment.

02

CAD and analytical design

We developed the assembly in SolidWorks, analyzed gear loading, shaft stresses, fatigue and bearing reactions, and revised the design to account for material and manufacturing constraints.

03

Fabrication and validation

We printed the components, prepared the shafts, checked the fits and assembled the gear trains before testing the shifting mechanism with a manual crank.

Manufacturing & troubleshooting

Manufacturing challenges and design changes

Challenge Response
Shaft strength and cost We revised the 20 mm shaft concept to a 40 mm hollow aluminum design and re-evaluated stress.
Helical gear loading We reduced the helix angle from approximately 45° to 15° to address calculated bending loads.
Dimensional variation We adjusted printed fits and manually refined the sourced aluminum shafts after measuring variation of approximately 0.05–0.25 mm against a specified ±0.01 mm tolerance.
Printability and material use We used PLA housing walls instead of a full PETG enclosure; tuned gear prints to six wall loops and 10% infill.

Test results

All six gear modes demonstrated

During our hand-cranked bench tests, all six gear selections operated smoothly. We also observed the expected lockup when two gear pairs were engaged at once.

Our prototype demonstrated the shifting mechanism and motion transfer. We did not test it under full automotive torque or establish its durability or service life.

Lessons and next iteration

One of my main takeaways was the importance of measuring actual parts and accounting for print shrinkage before finalizing the CAD model. For a future version, I would use an alignment fixture during bonding and simplify the housing to reduce material use.
