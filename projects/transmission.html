Shift Happens
Six-speed manual transmission

A working, hand-cranked transmission demonstrator designed in SolidWorks and fabricated using 3D-printed components and aluminum shafts.

Our objective was to translate a gearbox concept into a physical assembly capable of demonstrating gear selection, motion transfer, and the relationship between speed and torque.

Engineering process

From concept to prototype

01

System definition

Mapped power transfer and gear selection, identified critical components, and reviewed potential failures involving shaft deflection, gear wear, and alignment.

02

CAD and analytical design

Developed the SolidWorks assembly and examined gear loading, shaft stresses, fatigue, bearing reactions, and manufacturability. Revised the design as material and geometric constraints emerged.

03

Fabrication and validation

Printed components, prepared shafts, verified fits, assembled the gear trains, and evaluated shifting with a manual crank.

Manufacturing & troubleshooting

Engineering decisions that mattered

ChallengeResponse
Shaft strength and costRevised the 20 mm shaft concept to a 40 mm hollow aluminum design and re-evaluated stress.
Helical gear loadingReduced the helix angle from approximately 45° to 15° to address calculated bending loads.
Dimensional variationAdjusted printed fits and manually refined the sourced aluminum shafts after measuring variation of approximately 0.05–0.25 mm against a specified ±0.01 mm tolerance.
Printability and material useUsed PLA housing walls instead of a full PETG enclosure; tuned gear prints to six wall loops and 10% infill.

Test results

All six modes demonstrated.

During hand-cranked bench testing, the team reported smooth operation for each gear selection. Simultaneous engagement of two gear pairs also produced the anticipated lockup.

The prototype demonstrated kinematic function; full automotive torque capacity, durability, and service-life performance were not experimentally established.

Lessons and next iteration

Prioritize measured part dimensions, account for print shrinkage in CAD, and use an alignment fixture when bonding components. A redesigned housing could reduce material consumption and simplify assembly.
