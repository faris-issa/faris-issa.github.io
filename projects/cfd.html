MAE 460 / Project 1

Internal Flow with Thermodynamics

Investigated the relationship between fluid motion, heat transfer, gravity and compressibility using ANSYS Fluent.

01 — Buoyancy-driven thermal convection

Modeled transient internal water flow using the Boussinesq approximation. Calculated reference properties from a water-density correlation and compared temperature and velocity fields under Earth and Moon gravity.

02 — Helical pipe heat transfer and viscous heating

Evaluated heated-wall flow over four inlet velocities and compared Fluent temperature-rise predictions with analytical hand calculations. A separate glycerin study examined viscous heating across four flow velocities.

03 — Transient compressible flow

Compared two pressure-driven chamber-flow cases with the same 200 Pa pressure difference but different absolute pressures. Used a transient time step of 5.0 × 10⁻⁵ s and examined density, temperature, axial velocity and Mach number at 0.01 s.

Result: Peak Mach numbers were approximately 0.064 and 0.124 for Cases 1 and 2, respectively. Both remained subsonic; the lower absolute pressure case produced a faster jet and stronger cooling.

MAE 460 / Project 2

Transient Multiphase Flow

Applied the Volume of Fluid (VOF) method to track phase interfaces and compare the effects of ventilation, material properties and gravity.

01 — Warehouse ventilation

Compared passive ventilation with a fresh-air inlet in a two-dimensional methane leakage model. Both configurations used the same structured quadrilateral mesh (8,400 elements), 0.01 s time step and 7 s simulation duration to isolate the effect of ventilation.

Simulation result

Approximately 16.5% lower D-index

At 7 s, the reported D-index decreased from approximately 0.121 to 0.101 with added ventilation. The relative reduction is calculated from these approximate report values, not from a physical experiment.

02 — Liquid droplets on an inclined plate

Compared engine oil and mercury using the same 30° inclined geometry, mesh, initial droplet shape and transient settings. Used laminar VOF to visualize how each liquid interface changed over 0.10 s.

Result: Mercury advanced and spread farther downslope than engine oil during the simulated interval. The study used 102,400 elements and a time step of 0.001 s for both cases.

03 — Free-surface water oscillation in a U-pipe

Modeled a three-dimensional U-pipe with unequal initial water levels and examined the oscillating air mass flow through the left opening. Compared Earth and Mars gravity with otherwise unchanged boundary conditions.
