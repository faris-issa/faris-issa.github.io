MAE 460 / Project 1

Internal Flow with Thermodynamics

In my first project, I investigated how fluid motion changes with heat transfer, gravity and compressibility.

01 — Buoyancy-driven thermal convection

I modeled transient water flow using the Boussinesq approximation, calculated the reference properties from a water-density correlation, and compared temperature and velocity fields under Earth and Moon gravity.

Parameter Value
Operating temperature 308.15 K
Operating density 994.03 kg/m³
Thermal expansion coefficient 3.458 × 10⁻⁴ K⁻¹
Earth / Moon gravity magnitude 9.81 / 1.62 m/s²

At 60 seconds, I observed a more pronounced downward cold-water plume under Earth gravity. Under Moon gravity, the plume followed a shallower trajectory because of reduced buoyancy.

02 — Helical pipe heat transfer and viscous heating

I simulated heated-wall flow at four inlet velocities and compared the predicted temperature rise with my hand calculations. I also ran a separate glycerin study to examine viscous heating.

Inlet velocity (m/s) Fluent ΔT (K) Hand calculation ΔT (K)
0.01 13.88 13.589
0.02 7.06 6.795
0.04 3.60 3.397
0.08 1.83 1.699

As inlet velocity increased, the predicted temperature rise decreased. The largest absolute difference between my Fluent results and hand calculations was 0.291 K.

With viscous heating enabled, I found that the predicted temperature rise in glycerin increased from 0.032 K at 1.5 m/s to 0.400 K at 12 m/s.

03 — Transient compressible flow

I compared two pressure-driven chamber-flow cases with the same 200 Pa pressure difference but different absolute pressures. I used a time step of 5.0 × 10⁻⁵ s and examined density, temperature, axial velocity and Mach number at 0.01 s.

The peak Mach numbers were approximately 0.064 and 0.124 for Cases 1 and 2. Both remained subsonic, and the lower absolute pressure case produced a faster jet and stronger cooling.

MAE 460 / Project 2

Transient Multiphase Flow

For my second project, I used the Volume of Fluid (VOF) model to track phase interfaces and investigate the effects of ventilation, fluid properties and gravity.

01 — Warehouse ventilation

I compared passive ventilation with an additional fresh-air inlet in a two-dimensional methane leakage model. To isolate the effect of ventilation, I kept the structured quadrilateral mesh (8,400 elements), time step (0.01 s) and simulation duration (7 s) the same in both cases.

Simulation result

Approximately 16.5% lower D-index

At 7 s, the simulated D-index dropped from approximately 0.121 to 0.101 with the added ventilation. Using these approximate values, I calculated a relative reduction of 16.5%.

02 — Liquid droplets on an inclined plate

I compared engine oil and mercury on a 30° inclined plate using the same geometry, mesh, starting droplet shape and transient settings. I used laminar VOF to observe how the liquid interfaces changed over 0.10 s.

In my simulations, mercury traveled and spread farther downslope than engine oil. I used 102,400 elements and a 0.001 s time step for both cases.

03 — Free-surface water oscillation in a U-pipe

I modeled a three-dimensional U-pipe with unequal initial water levels and used the air mass-flow rate through the left opening to examine the oscillation. I compared Earth and Mars gravity while keeping the other boundary conditions unchanged.

Quantity Earth Mars
Gravity magnitude 9.81 m/s² 3.72 m/s²
Oscillation period (reported) 1.0 s 1.7 s
Mesh elements 13,806 13,806

I found that reducing gravity increased the oscillation period, consistent with the expected inverse-square-root relationship with gravity. The velocity contours also showed the flow reversing direction between the two legs.
